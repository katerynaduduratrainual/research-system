ICBL-Landmine Monitor Statement On International Cooperation And Assistance
Mine Ban Treaty Intersessional Meeting, Geneva, 15-18 June 2026

Thank you Chair,

2025 was a tumultuous year in many respects. The funding crisis impacting mine action unfolded early in the year, when the United States imposed its funding freeze, halting US-funded mine action activities in many countries. While some programs received waivers and permission to continue, others were unable to resume operations and were scaled back or terminated completely. The US has shifted its geographic funding priorities, reducing support to several States Parties, including Afghanistan, Angola, Colombia, the DRC, Iraq, Mali, Tajikistan, and Zimbabwe. The lack of predictability about future US funding and the setbacks caused by the funding freeze provided one of the biggest shocks to the mine action sector in recent years.

The US actions did not occur in isolation. Humanitarian and development aid budgets are being cut globally, and some traditional mine action donors, including States Parties to the Mine Ban Treaty, are reducing allocations as resources are redirected to other priorities, including increased defence spending.

Mine action funding is becoming increasingly concentrated in a small number of countries. Landmine Monitor data shows that, in 2024, 72% of all international assistance went to the ten largest recipients. Since 2022, a significant share has gone to Ukraine, while support to other States Parties has declined. Several affected States Parties, particularly in Sub-Saharan Africa, continue to struggle to secure funding. Chad, Guinea-Bissau, Mauritania, and Niger received minimal or no international assistance in 2024, and a similar trend is emerging in 2025. This lack of funding is slowing progress and contributing to repeated Article 5 extension requests, once the exception, but now the norm.

Despite growing humanitarian needs, victim assistance continues to be under funded within the mine action sector. Between 2020-2024, victim assistance dedicated funding represented just 5% of all international funding for the period. Many States Parties with significant victim needs continue to receive little or no support. The recent changes in US priorities and the closure and restructuring of USAID operations have further impacted services in many countries.

Budget cuts to mine action and humanitarian and development aid are placing communities at greater risk, pushing them towards more dangerous coping strategies in areas already affected by mine and ERW contamination, protracted conflict, climate change, and economic decline. Preliminary casualty data for 2025 released by the Monitor shows that the majority of countries with casualties in 2025, including most countries with over 100 casualties, were States Parties to the Mine Ban Treaty.

Article 6 obliges all States Parties in a position to do so to assist others in fulfilling their Treaty obligations. Sustained, well-coordinated donor support remains essential for affected States Parties to meet their commitments. In the face of overlapping crises, urgent action is needed to realize the ambitious vision of the Angkor-Siem Reap Action Plan for treaty implementation and universalization, grounded in national ownership and international cooperation.

All States Parties, donors and affected states alike, must take action to close funding gaps, ensure equitable distribution, and strengthen international cooperation. Donor States Parties should prioritize support that helps affected States Parties meet their Article 5 obligations and provide urgently needed assistance to victims. Cooperation among donors, and between donors and affected States Parties, should be strengthened, including through mechanisms such as the proposed Voluntary Trust Fund to support Article 5 implementation. Affected States Parties should keep Article 6 high on national agendas and maintain sustained national contributions.

Decisive action is needed in these uncertain times to ensure the Treaty continues to act on its promise to reduce the human suffering caused by landmines.

Thank you
